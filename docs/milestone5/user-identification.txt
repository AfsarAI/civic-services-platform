Project Name
Civic Services Platform – Pothole Complaint Management System

User Identification

The Civic Services Platform is designed to serve different categories of users involved in reporting, managing, and resolving pothole complaints. Based on the system requirements, the users are classified into Primary, Secondary, and Tertiary users.

------------------------------------------------------------

1. PRIMARY USER – CITIZEN

Description:
Citizens are the primary users of the Civic Services Platform. They use the platform to report potholes and other civic complaints, track complaint progress, receive notifications, and provide feedback after resolution.

Role:
Citizen

Responsibilities:
- Register and maintain their citizen account.
- Submit civic complaints with relevant details.
- Provide accurate complaint location using latitude and longitude.
- Provide complaint category, description, and supporting photographs where required.
- Track submitted complaints and their status.
- Review complaint details and complaint history.
- Provide feedback after a complaint is resolved.
- Review nearby complaints where applicable to identify possible duplicate complaints.

Goals:
- Report civic problems quickly and conveniently.
- Provide accurate location and useful complaint information.
- Track the progress of submitted complaints.
- Receive timely information when complaint status changes.
- Ensure reported problems are resolved and provide feedback on the resolution.

Pain Points:
- Difficulty reporting a complaint with accurate location information.
- Difficulty identifying the city and area associated with a PIN code.
- Lack of visibility into complaint progress after submission.
- Repeated or duplicate complaints for the same civic issue.
- Lack of timely notification when complaint status changes.
- Difficulty providing feedback after a complaint is resolved.

Key Needs:
- Simple and secure registration and login.
- Easy complaint submission.
- Accurate GPS / latitude and longitude location capture.
- City and area identification using PIN code.
- Complaint tracking and history.
- Notifications for important complaint updates.
- Ability to provide feedback after resolution.

------------------------------------------------------------

2. SECONDARY USER – FIELD WORKER

Description:
Field Workers are operational users responsible for investigating and resolving civic complaints assigned to them by the Administrator.

Role:
Field Worker

Responsibilities:
- View complaints assigned to them.
- Review complaint details, photographs, and location.
- Investigate the reported civic issue.
- Update the status of assigned complaints.
- Record work and resolution details.
- Complete assigned complaints and provide appropriate resolution information.

Goals:
- Receive clear and actionable complaint assignments.
- Locate the reported issue accurately.
- Complete assigned civic work efficiently.
- Keep complaint status information up to date.
- Provide sufficient resolution information for administrative review.

Pain Points:
- Difficulty finding the exact location of a complaint.
- Incomplete or unclear complaint information.
- Difficulty identifying which complaints require action.
- Lack of a clear view of assigned work and status.
- Additional effort when complaint information is duplicated or incomplete.

Key Needs:
- Secure role-based access.
- Clear list of assigned complaints.
- Access to complaint location and details.
- Ability to update complaint status.
- Ability to record resolution information.


------------------------------------------------------------

3. TERTIARY USER – ADMINISTRATOR

Description:
Administrators are administrative users of the Civic Services Platform. The database contains a single administrator role, `administrator`. The administrator profile maintains an administrative level that determines the scope of administrative access: System or Municipal.

Role:
Administrator

Administrative Levels:
- System Administrator – system-level administration with platform-wide responsibilities.
- Municipal Administrator – municipal-level administration restricted to the assigned city.

Responsibilities:
System Administrator:
- Manage Municipal Administrator accounts.
- Manage system-level configuration and settings.
- Maintain platform-wide administrative information.
- Perform administrative activities that are not restricted to a particular city.
- Oversee platform-wide administrative functions.

Municipal Administrator:
- Manage citizens and Field Workers within the assigned city.
- Manage complaints belonging to the assigned city.
- Assign complaints to appropriate Field Workers.
- Monitor complaint status and operational progress.
- Review duplicate complaints within the permitted municipal scope.
- Generate municipal reports and export relevant data.
- Manage municipal operations within the assigned city.

Goals:
System Administrator:
- Maintain secure and reliable platform-wide administration.
- Ensure appropriate administrative users have access to the system.
- Maintain consistent system-wide configuration.
- Provide a controlled administrative foundation for municipal operations.

Municipal Administrator:
- Manage civic complaints efficiently within the assigned city.
- Ensure complaints are assigned to appropriate Field Workers.
- Monitor complaint resolution and operational progress.
- Maintain accurate city-level civic service information.
- Generate useful reports for municipal monitoring and decision-making.

Pain Points:
System Administrator:
- Managing administrators across multiple municipal areas.
- Maintaining consistent system configuration.
- Preventing unauthorized administrative access.
- Maintaining a clear separation between system-wide and municipal administration.

Municipal Administrator:
- Managing a large number of complaints within a city.
- Identifying duplicate complaints.
- Assigning complaints to appropriate Field Workers.
- Monitoring complaint status and resolution progress.
- Maintaining accurate city-specific citizen and Field Worker information.
- Preparing reports and exporting data for municipal review.
- Ensuring access is restricted to the authorized city.

Key Needs:
System Administrator:
- Secure administrative authentication.
- Platform-wide administrative access.
- Ability to manage Municipal Administrators.
- Ability to manage system-level settings.
- Access to appropriate administrative monitoring and reporting.

Municipal Administrator:
- Secure role-based authentication.
- City-scoped access to data.
- Ability to manage citizens and Field Workers for the assigned city.
- Complaint assignment and monitoring.
- Duplicate complaint visibility.
- Access to city-specific reports and analytics.
- CSV report export.
- Controlled access that prevents access to other cities.


4. ROLE AND ADMINISTRATIVE LEVEL MODEL

The final system contains three database/application roles:

- Citizen
- Field Worker
- Administrator

The Administrator role has two administrative levels maintained in the Administrator profile:

Administrator
    |
    +-- System Level
    |     Platform-wide administration
    |
    +-- Municipal Level
          City-scoped administration

The administrative level is an attribute of the Administrator profile; it is not a separate database role.

Citizen
    ↓
Creates and tracks civic complaints and provides feedback.

Field Worker
    ↓
Handles complaints assigned by the Administrator.

Administrator
    ↓
Manages civic operations according to the administrator's level:
    - System level: platform-wide administration
    - Municipal level: assigned-city administration
